# Emberveil — Longer Memory

The citadel still stands. Its basalt does not forgive, and neither do the people who once hid beneath it.

This is the court a Magic player walks into when they want the same long conversation they have with a Commander deck or a well-made companion, without needing a larger rulebook. The mana, the blockers, and the combat math are already familiar. What is new is that each leader remembers why they are here.

## The Night of the Eclipse

A century ago the harbor went dark an hour before the sun should have set. Iskar had already marked the date. The tide did not come in. The Sentinels sealed the lower vaults while the Dawnward was still bright enough to hold the refugees in sleep. Most of the city never saw the gates close. Those who did still argue about who gave the order.

The vaults held. The ward did not. One by one the marks failed. The people inside aged in dream. Outside, the ash settled and the houses that remained began to claim the keys.

## The Houses

Sun still speaks of the names etched on shields. It remembers who was promised shelter and who was turned away. Moon speaks of the promise written into the hinges: that no single hand would hold the gates. Neither house has opened the doors. Both have sent leaders to the basalt court.

Tala rode the last road that still led out. The stones burned under her. She has not forgiven the decision to close the gates, and she has not stopped counting the people who were left on the wrong side of them.

Vael kept the list. Every name he swore to protect is still inside his shield. Some of them are still sleeping. He does not know which.

Neri listens to the voices the city removed from its own records. The veil still carries them. She is learning which ones are worth answering.

Sylva planted briar in the ash of an orchard that burned the same night the gates closed. The roots have reached the walls. She says the orchard is not finished.

Orrun made the first hinges. When the Sentinels stopped answering to the houses, he returned with a hammer. He has not decided whether he is repairing the machines or ending them.

Iskar watched the reflection a year early. The tide is still late. He has been drawing water from the harbor and reading what it refuses to say.

## What Remains

The Sentinels still walk the outer courts. They do not open the doors. The Dawnward still burns in fragments. Three marks restored will light the stair; none of the houses agree on who should climb it first.

The claimants arrive with their own decks and their own reasons. The court remembers who stands at dawn. It does not yet remember who is allowed to leave. A player who has sat across from a thousand different Magic decks can sit down here, read a leader’s capability in the lobby, and still be learning what that leader will do on turn seven.
