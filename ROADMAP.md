# Emberveil Roadmap

The citadel does not expand quickly. Neither should the game that stands on its steps.

Emberveil is meant to be the Baldur’s Gate for Magic players: a place where the rules are small enough to learn in one evening, yet the leaders and their trees give the same kind of long conversation with a system that a well-built Commander deck or a companion-driven RPG provides. You recognize the mana, the blockers, and the combat math. What you stay for is the reason each leader is standing in the court and the path you grow them down.

## Now (what is already standing)

- Six leaders with distinct decks, strategies, and ability trees.
- Growth points earned every three turns. One path chosen per leader.
- Free-for-all, team, and co-op modes. Co-op carries the first story chapter: restore three Dawnward marks or lose the vault.
- Opening-hand redraw, turn timers, batch combat with blocking and reactions.
- Illustrated cards and a lobby that shows each leader’s capability and strategy.

## Near (next seasons of ash)

**More of the same citadel**
- Two additional leaders. One who never left the outer court. One who arrived after the eclipse and claims no house. Each arrives with a clear capability statement and a strategy that an experienced Magic player can read in thirty seconds and still be surprised by on turn six.
- New cards that support existing trees instead of replacing them. Tidebind variants. Briar that can be sacrificed. Foundry cards that do more than ramp. Every new card must be explainable in the same short language already used for the leaders.
- A second co-op chapter. The doors open a crack. The people inside are no longer only sleeping. The Sentinels begin to argue with each other.
- Team mode gains a single shared objective that both houses can contest or abandon. The Accord can still be broken on purpose.

**Systems that already exist, made clearer**
- Ability tree effects fully implemented for every node (Rush, Guard, scry, on-death sprites, multi-target damage).
- A short rules page inside the game that uses the same language as the leader descriptions. No separate glossary. A Magic player should be able to open it, recognize the combat and mana pieces, and immediately understand what is different.
- Spectator mode for finished matches. The basalt court keeps a record of who stood at dawn.

## Farther (the vaults open further)

**Story that continues without ending the game**
- Seasonal chapters that do not wipe progress. Growth and unlocked nodes persist across matches if the player chooses the same leader. The trees are personal, not temporary. This is the companion relationship: you return to the same leader and the court remembers what you unlocked.
- A third house that was written out of the city’s records. Neri already hears it. The other leaders do not yet agree it exists.
- Co-op that can be lost in more than one way. Restore the ward and still fail the people. Defeat the Sentinels and leave the doors sealed. The story remembers the choice.

**Play that stays small**
- Ranked free-for-all limited to the existing six leaders. No new cards until the current ones are understood.
- A private table mode where two players can run the same leader against each other. The court allows it. The houses do not.
- Card art and leader portraits finished for every card that currently exists before any new art is commissioned.

## What is not planned

The game does not need a seventh mode. It does not need daily quests. It does not need a shop that sells growth points. The citadel already has too many people arguing over who holds the keys. The roadmap should not add more arguments of that kind.

When the next leader arrives, the court will already know their name. The rest of the work is deciding whether the gates open or stay closed — and whether a Magic player who sits down for one match leaves with a leader they want to return to.
